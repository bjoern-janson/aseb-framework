# Adaptive Structural Evolution Boundary (ASEB)

## The mechanics of evolvability

ASEB is a framework for studying the conditions that allow adaptive systems to continue evolving.

The central question is not:

> how does a system optimize?

but:

> what determines whether a system can continue to change the mechanisms by which it adapts?

---

## Core hypothesis

Evolution is not limited by variation alone.

The deeper constraint is whether a system remains capable of:

- receiving meaningful feedback
- modifying its internal structure
- discovering new representations
- creating new transformation pathways

A system can become highly optimized while losing the ability to evolve.

ASEB studies the boundary between:

adaptation
↓
structural transformation
↓
continued evolvability

---

# The central distinction

## Improvement

A system performs better within its current structure.

ΔA > 0

Capability increases.

---

## Evolution

A system changes the structure that produces capability.

ΔΓ > 0

The generator changes.

---

## Evolvability

A system preserves the ability to generate future structural changes.

ΔE > 0

The system remains open to transformation.

---

# The ASEB principle

A system is evolutionarily viable when feedback can reach the structures that determine future behavior.

This requires three forms of permeability:

## Causal permeability (P_C)

Can external reality modify the system?

A closed system cannot adapt to errors it cannot receive.

---

## Epistemic permeability (P_E)

Can beliefs and models be revised?

A system that cannot update its representations becomes trapped.

---

## Ontological permeability (O_C)

Can the system change what categories and structures it operates over?

The deepest adaptations require changing the space of possible representations itself.

---

# Adaptive boundaries

Every adaptive system has boundaries determining:

- what information enters
- what changes are possible
- what structures can be modified
- what futures can be reached

ASEB studies these boundaries.

The key question:

> Is the boundary protecting stability, or preventing evolution?

---

# Representation and evolution

A system cannot optimize what it cannot distinguish.

Therefore:

distinction
↓
representation
↓
optimization
↓
evolution


The creation of new meaningful distinctions is a prerequisite for new adaptive possibilities.

---

# Computational phase boundaries

ASEB proposes that major capability transitions occur when systems cross structural thresholds.

A capability increase alone is insufficient.

A deeper transition occurs when constraints change:

representation
↓
reachable computation
↓
new adaptive regimes


These transitions are investigated as computational phase boundaries.

---

# Research goals

ASEB aims to develop:

- formal models of adaptive boundaries
- simulations of structural evolution
- measurable permeability metrics
- tests for evolutionary phase transitions
- benchmarks distinguishing optimization from genuine evolvability
